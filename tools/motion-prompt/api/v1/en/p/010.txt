Motion Prompt #010 · motion/overshoot

Meaning: Goes a little past the target, then settles back
Example request (adapt to your project): Give the gauge needle an overshoot: about 10% past the target, then back.
What this demo shows: A gauge needle swings past its target, comes back and settles.
Demo specification (independent of the example request):
- One pass per 4.7 s. Semicircular gauge (radius 128, stroke 26). Target is 0.7 of the sweep (126° from the left end).
- Starts at 0.25 s: value = 0.7 × (1 − e^(−4.6t)·cos(10.5t)). About 0.3 s later it is roughly 25% past the target, then it oscillates and settles on the target.
- The part beyond the target is drawn in coral. A yellow tick marks the target.
- The filled arc is a blue→mint gradient; the needle is white, width 9.
- Return: from 3.7 s, value = 0.7 × e^(−7.5t)·cos(8.5t) back to 0 (dipping slightly below 0 before it stops).
When to use it: Use it to give an arriving element energy and a springy feel.
